Before you go further

Nothing on this site is medical advice, a diagnosis, or a prescription. Talk to a licensed provider before starting anything, especially if you're combining multiple products or have an existing health condition.

Peptides and dietary supplements are regulated very differently, and this site treats them as different risk categories, not one bucket. Most peptides here are unapproved research chemicals typically sourced outside normal pharmacy channels. Supplements are legal, widely available products — the FDA doesn't pre-approve them before sale the way it does drugs, but they're a different, generally lower-risk category. Check the evidence tag on every entry either way; it reflects how strong the actual human research is, not how popular or legal the product is.

    Vetting checklist

    How to evaluate a source yourself

    We deliberately don't maintain a "trusted vendor" list — the market shifts constantly, and a wrong recommendation here isn't a broken link, it's someone injecting an unverified substance. Use this checklist on any vendor you're considering instead.

    • Third-party lab testing. Look for a Certificate of Analysis (COA) from an independent lab — not the vendor's own in-house test — ideally with HPLC or mass spectrometry results tied to the specific batch/lot number you'd receive, not a generic PDF reused across products.
    • Batch-specific results, not stock photos. A real COA references a lot number that matches what ships to you. If every product shares one generic "test result" image, treat that as a red flag.
    • Transparent business identity. A real company name, physical address, and contact information you can verify — not just a storefront with a contact form and no other footprint.
    • Independent reputation. Look for a track record across sources you don't control the search for — independent forums, long-running community discussions, or review aggregators — rather than testimonials on the vendor's own site.
    • No explicit medical claims. "Research use only" vendors making direct human-dosing or treatment claims are typically operating outside the labeling that keeps them in a legal gray area in the first place — that inconsistency is itself worth noting.
    • Clear policies. A stated return/refund policy, secure checkout, and no requirement to pay by untraceable methods (e.g. wire transfer, crypto-only with no alternative, gift cards).
    • Consistency over time. A vendor with years of consistent, verifiable testing and community track record is a different risk profile than one that's brand new or has recently changed names.
